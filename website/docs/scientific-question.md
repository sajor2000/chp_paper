# Scientific question and hypotheses

## Why the study is needed

Public-health datasets and health-system EHR data observe different populations
through different measurement systems. Population surveys and modeled small-area
estimates are designed for population inference. Chicago Health Map summarizes
diagnoses recorded during care among adults observed in participating health
systems.

Many public products report at ZIP-code or community-area scales. A single label
for a larger area can hide clinically recorded variation among the tracts inside
that area. The study therefore asks an information question: **does the smaller
geography add a materially different description?**

## Primary question

Among eligible Chicago census tracts during 2022–2024, do direct tract-level
Chicago Health Map measures contain geographic information beyond the direct
Chicago Health Map community-area label?

## Primary hypothesis

If tract resolution adds information, we expected to observe all or some of the
following:

- nonzero variation among tracts within the same community area;
- meaningful absolute differences between tract and community-area percentile ranks;
- disagreement between tract and community-area quartile classifications; and
- movement into or out of the highest tract quartile after applying the larger-area label.

Evidence against added tract resolution would be near-zero within-area variation,
small rank gaps, near-complete quartile agreement, and little movement across the
highest-quartile boundary.

## Secondary questions

- Does the conclusion change when Census ZCTA is the coarser geography?
- How closely do Chicago Health Map and CDC PLACES rank the same tracts?
- Do Chicago Health Map and the Healthy Chicago Survey similarly order community areas for hypertension?
- Is remaining spatial structure detectable after the supplementary ecological model?

## What was not hypothesized

The study did not hypothesize that Chicago Health Map estimates population
prevalence, identifies individual risk, establishes causality, measures unmet
need, locates services, or proves that one data source is correct.
