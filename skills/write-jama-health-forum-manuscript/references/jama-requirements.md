# JAMA Health Forum Requirements Snapshot

Source: [JAMA Health Forum Instructions for Authors](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama-health-forum/pages/instructions-for-authors), verified July 14, 2026. This is an orientation snapshot, not a compliance authority. Recheck the live page during every compliance or submission-readiness task. Follow AMA Manual of Style, 11th edition, ICMJE Recommendations, and the study-appropriate EQUATOR guideline.

## Original Investigation limits

| Element | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Main text | 3000 words |
| Structured abstract | No more than 350 words |
| Key Points | Question, Findings, Meaning; journal wording is "75-100 words or less"; do not exceed 100 words |
| Research title | No more than 100 characters including spaces |
| Main displays | No more than 5 tables and/or figures |
| References | 50-75 |
| Other | Data Sharing Statement and applicable reporting checklist |

## Title and file

- Make the title concise, specific, informative, and nondeclarative. Do not use a question or result direction.
- Do not include study design in titles of observational reports. Trials and meta-analyses follow their specific subtitle rules.
- Do not use abbreviations in the title or abstract.
- Submit a Word manuscript using 10-, 11-, or 12-point type, double spacing, and a ragged right margin.
- Place Key Points before the Abstract.

## Structured abstract

Use separate submission headings: Importance; Objective; Design; Setting; Participants; Intervention(s) or Exposure(s); Main Outcome(s) and Measure(s); Results; Conclusions and Relevance. JAMA may combine Design, Setting, and Participants during editing after acceptance.

- Begin Results with the analyzed sample and key demographic characteristics.
- Quantify primary outcomes using absolute values or rates and uncertainty. Do not report a P value alone.
- Include absolute quantities with ratio measures.
- Keep conclusions supported by abstract results; add no new outcome or interpretation.

## Key Points

- Write Question in 1 sentence.
- Write Findings in 1 or 2 sentences; name the design and primary result using basic numbers and state whether results were statistically significant or not significant when applicable.
- Omit CIs, variance, P values, test statistics, and secondary outcomes from Findings.
- Write Meaning in 1 sentence and keep its implication proportional to the primary result.

## Methods and statistics

- State objective or hypothesis, design, setting, dates, selection, eligibility, sources, exposures, outcomes, and essential methods.
- Describe methods sufficiently for a knowledgeable reader with the data to reproduce the analysis.
- End Methods with tests, a priori significance or CI levels, sidedness, software version/manufacturer, and relevant packages.
- Report model variables, transformations, clustering/repeated-measure handling, diagnostics, and fit where available.
- Explain race and ethnicity source, classification, categories, and analytic rationale. List categories alphabetically when appropriate.
- Describe IRB approval, exemption, waiver, and consent as applicable.
- Treat secondary multiplicity or unplanned analyses as exploratory or hypothesis-generating.

## Results and language

- Report the number of observations for observational studies.
- Quantify findings with uncertainty; do not rely on hypothesis testing alone.
- Avoid nontechnical use of statistical terms such as correlation, random, significant, and trend.
- Use causal language, including effect and efficacy, only where current JAMA policy and the design permit it. Describe nonrandomized work using association or correlation language.
- Report every primary outcome identified in Methods with exact values and uncertainty in the Abstract and text or a table, not only in a figure.

## Tables, figures, and supplement

- Number displays in citation order, give each a brief descriptive title, cite each in text, and verify values across all artifacts.
- Prefer titles of 10-15 words. Build tables in editable cells, with comparisons reading horizontally.
- Report frequency data as `No. (%)`; include numerators and denominators when possible. Explain empty cells, suppression, and totals that do not sum.
- Focus regression tables on primary unadjusted and adjusted comparisons; move extended models to the supplement.
- Do not duplicate the same data in a table and figure.
- Do not use pie charts or 3-dimensional graphs. Use bar charts for frequencies; avoid stacked bars except for justified ordinal distributions.
- Label axes and units. Define symbols, line styles, colors, and error bars. Ensure colors remain distinguishable.
- Limit complex multipart figures. Submit main figures separately when required; cite and number all eTables/eFigures in order.

## Human and AI accountability

Authors remain responsible for accuracy and originality. Current instructions require disclosure of qualifying AI or language-model assistance, including tool/model, version, manufacturer, dates, content created or edited, and author responsibility. Do not use language models to generate or format references. Verify references through source records and a reference manager.
