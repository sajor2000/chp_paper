# Human JAMA-Style Scientific Writing

## Core voice

Write compressed, concrete, policy-relevant prose. Preserve human judgment and rhythm: vary sentence roles, use accountable verbs, and let emphasis come from evidence rather than adjectives.

- Aim for 18-28 words in most sentences as an editing heuristic, not a journal rule. Review sentences longer than 40 words.
- Put a concrete subject and verb early: `We estimated`, `The proportion differed`, or `[N] areas met the threshold`.
- Use active voice for investigator choices and passive voice when the actor is irrelevant, as in `Cells were suppressed`.
- Prefer verbs to nominalizations: `We compared the measures`, not `We conducted a measure-comparison assessment`.
- Keep technical names stable, then use an unambiguous short form. Do not rotate synonyms for variety.
- Follow a dense estimate sentence with a short boundary when needed.
- End a paragraph when its claim changes. Many published paragraphs contain 3-6 sentences, but argument controls length.

## Mechanical-language screen

Review rather than automatically delete: `Importantly`, `Notably`, `Interestingly`, `Taken together`, `These findings underscore`, `It is important to note`, `valuable lens`, and `may help inform`. Replace each with the specific relationship or implication it hides.

Avoid decorative triples, repetitive four-sentence templates, unclear `this/these/it`, anthropomorphized data, and repeated claims of novelty. Read the manuscript aloud and rewrite any technically correct sentence that the authors would not naturally say.

## Section movement

### Introduction

Move from consequential policy or public-health problem, to established evidence, to the exact gap, to one objective sentence. Cite factual background and prior evidence. Avoid generic opening claims, premature methods, and unsupported first/unique/comprehensive claims.

### Methods

Follow executable order: design/source; population/setting; exposure and outcome definitions; missingness/reliability; analysis; ethics/reporting. Define a rule before describing who passed it. Keep decisions that change interpretation in the main text.

### Results

Move from analyzed denominator and exclusions, to description, to primary estimate and uncertainty, to secondary/sensitivity findings. Match Methods and display order. Separate result from interpretation. Report absolute quantities with relative measures.

Pattern: `Among [N] observations, [n] ([%]) met [criterion]. [Exposure] was associated with [outcome] ([estimate]; 95% CI, [lower] to [upper]) (Table 2).`

### Discussion

Open with principal finding, inference boundary, and proportional relevance. Compare with prior work only after stating the present result. Distinguish supported interpretation from plausible mechanism. Write limitations as threat, consequence or direction when knowable, and remaining uncertainty. End with 2 or 3 conclusion sentences and no new result.

### Abstract and Key Points

Write these after the main text and displays are frozen. Compress; do not broaden. Make every abstract number resolve to the same source as the corresponding Results sentence. Keep Meaning narrower than Conclusions and Findings narrower than Results.

## Short published craft exemplars

Use these supplied JAMA Health Forum sentences to study function, not to copy wording or imitate an author's distinctive expression. Each excerpt is short and retained solely for local scholarly style analysis.

[Barsky et al, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamahealthforum.2026.2059): "People aged 35 years or older had higher ORs than people aged 18 to 34 years, and females had a lower OR than males."

The parallel comparison identifies groups and directions without commentary.

[Jiang et al, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamahealthforum.2026.2232): "Cost sharing can deter utilization of high-value services such as primary care." The concrete subject and verb make an efficient Discussion topic sentence.

## Human revision sequence

1. Read paragraph-opening sentences as a reverse outline.
2. Read aloud for breath, cadence, and disappearing grammatical subjects.
3. Resolve unclear pronouns and repeated openings.
4. Remove stock phrases and unsupported intensifiers.
5. Verify every number, sign, unit, denominator, CI level, and direction.
6. Have a subject-matter author restore the team's natural voice without loosening claim boundaries.
